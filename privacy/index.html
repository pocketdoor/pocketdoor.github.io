Pocket Door privacy policy

Effective 19 November 2026

In short: Pocket Door links your Android phone to your own Windows PC. What it reads on your phone goes only to the PC you linked, straight over your Wi-Fi, encrypted. There are no Pocket Door servers and no accounts. Nothing is sold, shared or used for ads. Each feature is off until you turn it on, and you can turn it off any time.

How Pocket Door works

Pocket Door has two parts: an app on your Android phone and an app on your Windows PC. You link them once by scanning a code shown on the PC. After that, the PC connects to the phone over your home Wi-Fi. Everything between them is encrypted, and each side only talks to the other one it was linked with. Your data never goes through the internet or through anyone else's computer, including ours.

What the phone app reads, and where it goes

Each one is off until you turn it on. The app's setup screens and Settings say what each one reads before Android asks you.

Notifications

With Android's notification access, Pocket Door reads the notifications on your phone (which app, the title, the text, pictures and buttons) and sends them to your linked PC, so you can read and answer them there. Replies and button presses you make on the PC are sent back to the phone. For a Gmail notification, the PC is also told which of your Gmail accounts it belongs to, so clicking it opens the right inbox. In Settings › Notifications, you can turn off individual apps so their notifications stay on your phone; Pocket Door remembers which apps have sent notifications on your phone only and never sends that list to the PC.

Text messages

If you turn on Text messages, Pocket Door reads the texts on your phone (who sent them and what they say) so they show on your PC, including texts Android hides from notification readers, and so you can open a whole conversation from the PC (up to its 50 most recent texts). Texts go only to your linked PC.

Calls

If you turn on Calls, Pocket Door uses Android's Phone, Call logs and Contacts permissions only to see that your phone is ringing and who is calling (their number, and their name and picture from your contacts). That goes to your PC while the call rings. Pocket Door does not read your past calls or your contacts list.

While Notifications or Calls is on, the PC is also told when a call is under way and when it ends (not who it's with), so it can pause its music and mute its microphone during the call. With both off, the PC isn't told about calls at all.

Photos and videos

If you allow photos, your PC can see the photos and videos on your phone, including where they were taken, so copies saved on the PC match the originals. They're sent to your PC when you look at them or save them there. You can allow only some photos instead.

Files

If you turn on Files, Pocket Door uses Android's "All files access" so you can browse the files and folders on your phone's storage from your PC. Files are read and sent to the PC only when you open, copy or save them there.

Clipboard and Accessibility

Text you copy on the PC can be pasted on the phone, and the other way round. To send what you copy on the phone without a tap, Pocket Door can use Android's Accessibility access, only if you turn it on. When you tap something, a menu or window opens, or a pop-up or notification appears in another app, Android shows Pocket Door its words. Pocket Door looks only for words like Copy or Copied. When it sees one, Android lets only the app in front read the clipboard, so Pocket Door opens an invisible window for a moment, reads what you copied and closes the window. What you copied goes to your linked PC. A copy an app marks as private, like a password, goes only while Send copied passwords is on (it is never shown on screen and is kept out of Windows' clipboard history), and you can turn Send copied passwords off in Settings › Copied text. The words are checked on the phone and thrown away. Without Accessibility, you send what you copied with the Send clipboard button instead.

Things you send yourself

Files, photos, text and links you send with Share › Send to PC, or from the app, go to your linked PC. Files and folders sent from the PC are saved in your phone's Downloads folder.

About the phone itself

The PC is told the phone's name and battery level, so it can show them, and a random number made when the app was installed, so it knows it's the same phone next time.

Other permissions

  • Nearby devices (local network): so your PC can reach the phone on your Wi-Fi.
  • Notifications: for Pocket Door's own notifications (for example, a file arriving from the PC).
  • Companion device link: on phones that hide some notifications from apps like this one, you can link your PC as a companion device in Android. The PC sends a short Bluetooth signal so the phone can find it.
  • Exact alarms and media playback: so "Ring my phone" from the PC can be heard, even when the phone is locked or on silent.
  • Start at boot: so the link to your PC comes back after the phone restarts.
  • Running in the background: if you allow it, Android doesn't pause Pocket Door to save battery, so your PC can always reach your phone.
  • Installing updates: when you choose to update Pocket Door on your phone, Android asks you once to let Pocket Door install updates for itself.

What is kept, and where

On the phone: the keys that link it to your PCs, your choices in Settings, which text conversations your PC may open, and up to 20 recent non-password items received from your PC (copied text and the names and locations of received files) so your Recent list stays after a restart. Passwords are never saved to storage, and tapping Clear removes the list. It also keeps a small log of what the app did, which never includes what your notifications, texts, copies or files say or are called (if you tap Contact support, you can choose whether to attach that log to your email). None of this is backed up or copied to a new phone.

On the PC: your settings, the link keys, any files still waiting to send to your phone, notification pictures (app icons and senders' pictures, so Windows can show them), and a log of what the app did. The log can include app names and the names of files you move, but not what your messages say. Photos and files you open or drag from the phone are copied to a temporary folder, and ones you save go where you save them.

Google's QR code scanner

To link a PC, the phone app uses Google's code scanner, part of Google Play services, so Pocket Door never needs your camera. Scanning happens on the phone: the picture and the code aren't sent to Google. Google's scanner does send Google some technical details for diagnostics, such as the phone model, Android version, the app's name and version, and how fast scanning was. See Google's ML Kit data disclosure.

Updates

Twice a day, each app asks GitHub, where Pocket Door's downloads are kept, whether there's a newer version. On your PC, updates install automatically unless you turn that off in Settings; on your phone, Pocket Door lets you know and only downloads an update when you tap Update. That check carries nothing about you or your phone; like any web request, GitHub sees the address it came from. Aside from Google's QR scanner above, it's the only time either app contacts anything besides your own phone and PC.

What Pocket Door does not do

  • It has no servers, accounts or sign-in.
  • It has no ads, analytics or crash-reporting services.
  • It never sells or shares your data, and never uses it for advertising.
  • It doesn't collect your location, apart from the location already stored in photos you choose to share with your own PC.

Your choices and deleting your data

  • Each feature can be turned off in Pocket Door's Settings on the phone, and Android's permissions can be taken back in Android's settings.
  • Unlink a PC on your phone, or unlink your phone on your PC, to stop them sharing.
  • Uninstalling Pocket Door from the phone removes everything it kept there. Uninstalling it from the PC removes its settings, log and copies there. Files you saved stay where you saved them.

Children

Pocket Door is not made for children under 13.
